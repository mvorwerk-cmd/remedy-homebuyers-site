How to Sell a Distressed House in Cleveland Without a Realtor

By Matt Vorwerk October 28, 2026 8 min read

Yes, you can sell a distressed house in Cleveland without a realtor: Ohio does not require a homeowner to hire an agent. You will need a valid deed, a title company or attorney to close, the Ohio property disclosure form in most cases, and a buyer who accepts the house as-is. The simplest no-agent routes are selling directly to a cash buyer or selling it yourself.

If the roof leaks, the city has sent you a violation notice, or the house has sat empty since a parent passed, listing it the usual way can feel impossible. Below is what Cleveland and Ohio actually require, what it costs, and four honest options, including when a cash sale is not your best move.

Key Takeaways

  • Ohio law does not require a seller to use a real estate agent; you do need a properly signed and notarized deed and a closing through a title company or attorney.
  • Most Ohio sellers of 1-4 unit homes must give buyers the disclosure form under ORC 5302.30, but court-ordered, foreclosure, fiduciary and some inherited transfers are exempt.
  • Cleveland requires vacant properties to register: $70 per unit for 1-3 unit homes (Ordinance 3106). The mandatory pre-sale exterior inspection ended February 6, 2026.
  • Cuyahoga County charges a $0.50 transfer fee plus $4.00 per $1,000 conveyance fee on a deed transfer.
  • A cash offer is below retail, so it fits best when condition, speed or certainty matter more than top price.

Can You Legally Sell a House in Cleveland Without a Realtor?

In Ohio, a homeowner may sell their own property without a licensed agent. What the law cares about is that the transfer is done correctly: the seller must actually hold title, the deed must be signed and acknowledged before a notary under ORC 5301.01, and the county must record it.

In practice, even a sale with no agent runs through a title company or real estate attorney. They search the title, collect payoff amounts for any mortgage, tax or lien, prepare closing paperwork, and record the deed with the Cuyahoga County Fiscal Office. Expect to pay for title services, but a direct cash buyer typically covers them.

What Counts as a Distressed House in Cleveland?

A distressed house is one with problems that make a normal retail sale hard: major repairs, open code violations, back taxes, a pending foreclosure, a tenant who will not leave, or an estate that has not been settled. Cleveland has many older homes, and in our experience the biggest hurdles are rarely the repairs themselves. They are the liens, unsettled title, and city paperwork stacked on top.

Before choosing a path, collect four facts: who is on the deed, what you owe on it (mortgage, taxes, liens), whether the city has open violations, and whether anyone lives there. Those four answers decide which option below is realistic.

What Must You Disclose When Selling in Ohio?

Under ORC 5302.30, a person transferring residential property of one to four units must complete the state's disclosure form and give each prospective buyer a signed, dated copy "as soon as is practicable." The form covers known issues such as water leaks, structural problems, roof condition and wiring.

If the buyer receives the form after signing a purchase agreement, the buyer may rescind within three business days of receiving it, and no later than the earlier of 30 days after the offer is accepted or the closing date. Skipping the form does not avoid the problem; it gives the buyer a way out.

Who is exempt from the disclosure form?

The statute exempts several transfers, including court-ordered transfers such as probate orders, foreclosure sales, transfers by a fiduciary of an estate, guardianship or trust, transfers to a spouse or lineal relatives, and transfers by a seller who inherited the property and did not live there within the year before the transfer. Exempt does not mean you can hide known defects: Ohio fraud rules still apply.

What Are Cleveland's Rules for Vacant and Code-Violation Houses?

If your house is empty, Cleveland's Ordinance 3106 requires it to be registered as a vacant property. According to the City of Cleveland's vacant property page, the annual fee is $70 per unit for 1-3 unit homes. Commercial and industrial structures, including 4+ unit residential buildings, pay $1,000 and may need a cash bond of $5,000 or $15,000 depending on size. Fines for violations run $200 to $1,000 per offense, with each day counted separately.

One recent change helps sellers. Since February 6, 2026, the city no longer requires a mandatory exterior inspection before the sale or transfer of a vacant 1-3 unit residential property. Registration is still required, and routine city inspections and enforcement continue.

Code violations are charged to the owner of record, so ask the Cleveland Department of Building and Housing what is open before you set a price. A buyer can purchase a house with violations as-is, but you will need to disclose what you know, and any liens or fines will show up on the title search. What Does It Cost to Sell and Transfer a Deed in Cuyahoga County?

The Cuyahoga County Fiscal Office lists a transfer fee of $0.50 plus a conveyance fee of $4.00 for every $1,000 of the purchase price, with a $4.00 minimum, collected when the deed is filed with a statement of value (the DTE 100 form). On a $60,000 sale, that conveyance fee is $240. If the form shows no price, the fee is based on the home's market value as set by the cashier.

Other costs to plan for: payoff of any mortgage, back property taxes and liens, title and escrow charges, and any repairs you choose to make. Federal tax may apply to your profit; IRS Publication 523 explains the home-sale exclusion of up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for a qualifying joint return), which many distressed sellers will not owe anything on.

Option A: Sell directly to a cash buyer

A direct buyer purchases as-is: no repairs, no cleaning, no showings, and no commission. Closing can happen in a couple of weeks, or on a date you choose. The honest trade-off is price. The buyer takes on repairs and risk, so the offer lands below what a renovated house might bring. Ask any buyer to show how they reached the number, and compare more than one offer.

Option B: Sell it yourself

For a house in livable shape, FSBO can net more because there is no commission. You will price it, list it, host showings, review offers, and manage buyer inspections and appraisals. Many buyers use financing, and a distressed property can fail a lender's appraisal, so deals fall through. Expect to hire an attorney or title company anyway.

Option C: Repair it, then sell

If repairs are cosmetic or limited, a modest fix-up can widen your buyer pool. Get written bids first. If the number is more than you can fund, or the repairs are structural, skipping the work and selling as-is is often the better financial call.

Option D: Donate to the land bank

The Cuyahoga Land Bank has accepted donations of unwanted single- and two-family houses, but it does not accept occupied properties and can decline any application, based on whether the home can be rehabilitated or otherwise made productive. See its property donations page. It is a last resort for a house that has no market value.

What If the House Is in Foreclosure, Probate or Has a Tenant?

These situations change the playbook. If you are behind on your mortgage, read our stop foreclosure guide and our Ohio foreclosure article first, since timing is critical. If the owner has died, only the executor or administrator appointed by the probate court can sign the deed, so see our probate guide. With a tenant, the buyer takes the property subject to the lease unless it ends, so tell any buyer upfront.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a real estate agent to sell my house in Cleveland?

No. Ohio does not require a homeowner to hire a licensed agent to sell their own house. You do need a valid deed, a title company or attorney to close, and, for most sales, the Ohio Residential Property Disclosure Form. Selling to a cash buyer or on your own just shifts those tasks to you and the title company.

Do I have to fix code violations before selling a distressed house in Cleveland?

Not necessarily. Violations are tied to the property and its owner of record, and a buyer can agree to take a house as-is. But you must still disclose known problems, and unpaid fines or liens will surface at closing. Ask the Cleveland Department of Building and Housing for open violations before you set a price.

What is the Ohio property disclosure form and who is exempt?

Under ORC 5302.30, sellers of one-to-four-unit homes generally give buyers a signed disclosure form about known defects. Exemptions include court-ordered transfers, foreclosure sales, fiduciary transfers by an estate or trust, and heirs who did not live in the home within the past year. If a buyer never gets the form, the buyer can rescind.

What does it cost to transfer a deed in Cuyahoga County?

The Cuyahoga County Fiscal Office lists a $0.50 transfer fee plus a conveyance fee of $4.00 per $1,000 of the sale price, with a $4.00 minimum, filed with a DTE 100 form. On a $60,000 sale that conveyance fee is $240. Your title company will confirm the exact amounts at closing.

Is a cash offer always the best way to sell a distressed Cleveland house?

No. A cash offer is below what a repaired and well-marketed house can bring, because the buyer takes on repairs and risk. It tends to make sense when the house needs major work, you are behind on payments or taxes, or you need a firm closing date. If the house is in decent shape and you have time, listing usually nets more.
