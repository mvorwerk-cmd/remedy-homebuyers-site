Behind on Property Taxes in Chicago? Cook County Tax Sale Rules and Your Options

By Matt Vorwerk November 2, 2026 8 min read

If you are behind on property taxes in Chicago, the county will not take your house right away. Cook County first adds interest, then sells your unpaid tax bill to an investor at the Annual Tax Sale, and you keep the right to redeem it, generally for up to 2.5 years. You can pay in full, use a payment plan, or sell the house and pay the taxes from the proceeds.

Falling behind on taxes is stressful, and it is more common than people think, especially after a job loss, an illness, or the death of a family member who handled the bills. This guide explains the Cook County process in plain English, what each option costs, and when selling to a cash buyer is, and is not, the right move.

Key Takeaways

  • Cook County sells overdue tax bills to investors at the Annual Tax Sale; the sale transfers a lien, not your deed.
  • Under 35 ILCS 200/21-350, the general redemption period runs up to 2.5 years from the sale date, with shorter periods for some property types.
  • Late interest on recent Cook County bills is 0.75% per month (9% a year); older delinquent years carry 1.5% per month, according to local reporting.
  • The Treasurer offers a payment plan, but it only prevents the sale if every payment is made as scheduled.
  • You can sell with back taxes owed; the title company pays them from your proceeds at closing.

What Happens When You Fall Behind on Property Taxes in Chicago?

In Cook County, property tax is billed in two installments, and an unpaid installment becomes delinquent after its due date. Late bills accrue interest every month. As reported by ABC7 Chicago, interest on recent bills runs 0.75% per month, which is 9% a year, while taxes due in 2023 and earlier still accrue at 1.5% per month.

That interest is why a small unpaid bill can become a large one. A bill that would have cost $4,000 on time grows by roughly $30 a month at the lower rate, and twice that at the older rate. Interest figures can change with state law, so confirm the number for your bill on the Cook County Treasurer's website.

How Does the Cook County Annual Tax Sale Work?

The Cook County Annual Tax Sale is a yearly auction of prior-year delinquent taxes, not of the houses themselves. According to the Cook County Treasurer, investors bid on the right to pay the overdue taxes, and the winning bidder receives a lien on the property. The sale takes place roughly 13 months after the second installment due date.

Once your taxes are offered, you must pay the overdue amount and interest in one lump sum. Bidders compete by offering to accept the lowest interest rate from you, so the rate is set by the auction. The investor, called the tax buyer, then waits to be repaid when you redeem. If you never redeem, the tax buyer can eventually ask the court for a tax deed, which is why the redemption deadline matters so much.

What is the Scavenger Sale?

The Scavenger Sale is a separate, less frequent sale for properties with several years of unpaid taxes. The Treasurer describes it as an auction of taxes on properties delinquent for three or more years, held every two years. See the Treasurer's Scavenger Sale page for property owners. If your house has been behind for years, assume this sale is a real possibility and act early.

How Long Do You Have to Redeem After a Tax Sale in Cook County?

Illinois law gives owners a redemption period after a tax sale. 35 ILCS 200/21-350 sets the general limit at 2.5 years from the date of the sale, and the Illinois statutes provide shorter windows for certain property, such as vacant non-farm land and buildings of seven or more residential units. Redeeming means paying the overdue taxes plus the interest and fees that built up since the sale.

Redemption payments in Cook County go through the County Clerk, which publishes the process on its tax redemption payments page. The cost rises with time, so redeeming sooner is cheaper. Ask the Clerk for an exact payoff figure for your parcel rather than estimating.

Can You Get a Payment Plan for Delinquent Taxes in Cook County?

Yes. The Cook County Treasurer runs a Payment Plan Calculator for delinquent balances over $100, with monthly or twice-monthly installments. For late second-installment bills, the Treasurer announced a 13-month plan at 9% annual interest, according to ABC7 Chicago's report.

The plan has limits. Enrolling for one tax year does not cover the next year, and making only some payments does not exempt your property from the Annual Tax Sale. But if you make every payment on schedule, the taxes are paid in full before the sale starts. In our experience, a plan works well for owners with steady income and a short-term gap, and poorly for those whose finances are not recovering.

Whichever you choose, start by getting two numbers: your exact delinquent balance from the Treasurer and, if your taxes were already sold, the redemption amount from the County Clerk. Then compare them to what the house could sell for.

Can You Sell a House in Chicago If You Owe Back Property Taxes?

Yes, you can sell a Chicago house with back taxes owed. During closing, the title company calculates what is owed to the county or the tax buyer and pays it from your proceeds before you receive anything. Owing taxes only blocks a sale if the price does not cover the debts on the property.

That makes equity the deciding fact. If the house is worth well above the taxes and any mortgage, you have room to sell on your own terms. If the house needs major work and the debts are close to its value, a quick as-is sale may still beat watching interest grow, but it will be tighter. Compare at least two offers, and ask any buyer to show you how they got their number.

An honest note on cash offers

A cash offer is below what a repaired, well-marketed house can sell for, because the buyer takes on repairs and risk. We tell sellers when listing makes more sense. If you have equity, time, and a house in decent shape, listing or a payment plan will often leave you with more money. Cash makes sense when speed, condition or certainty is the real problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I don't pay my property taxes in Chicago?

Cook County adds interest to late bills, and once a bill stays unpaid long enough it is offered at the Annual Tax Sale. Investors bid on the overdue taxes, not on your house, and the winner receives a lien. You can still redeem by paying the taxes, interest and fees before the redemption period ends.

How long do I have to redeem after a Cook County tax sale?

Under 35 ILCS 200/21-350, the general redemption period is up to 2.5 years from the date of the tax sale, with shorter periods for some property types, such as vacant land. The amount owed grows over time, so the Cook County Clerk's redemption office should confirm your exact payoff for your parcel.

Can I sell my Chicago house if I owe back property taxes?

Yes. Owing back taxes does not stop a sale. The overdue taxes, interest and any tax-buyer redemption amount are paid from your sale proceeds at closing, and a title company will calculate the payoff. The key limit is equity: the sale price must cover what you owe for you to walk away with cash.

Does a Cook County payment plan stop the tax sale?

Only if you complete it. The Treasurer's payment plan calculator lets you pay a delinquent balance in installments, but it does not exempt a property from the sale on its own. If you make every payment as scheduled, the taxes are fully paid before the sale begins.

Is selling to a cash buyer a good idea if I owe back taxes?

Sometimes. A cash buyer can close quickly and pay off taxes at closing, but the offer is below retail value. If you have equity, time, and a house in decent shape, a listing or a payment plan may leave you with more. A cash sale fits best when speed or condition is the real problem.
