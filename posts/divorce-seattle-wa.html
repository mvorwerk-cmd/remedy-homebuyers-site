Divorce and Your Seattle Home: How Washington Splits Real Estate

By Matt Vorwerk October 21, 2026 8 min read

When you divorce in Seattle, Washington courts divide the family home in a way that is "just and equitable" under RCW 26.09.080, which is not always 50/50. The house can be sold and split, one spouse can buy the other out, or one spouse can keep it with an offsetting award. Which path fits depends on equity, the mortgage, and your timeline.

Divorce is hard enough without a house decision attached, and a Seattle mortgage can feel like a third party in the room. Below is how Washington's rules work, what taxes apply, and four honest options for the home, including when selling to a cash buyer like us is not your best move.

Key Takeaways

  • Washington is a community property state, but the court's standard is "just and equitable," not an automatic 50/50 split (RCW 26.09.080).
  • No divorce can be finalized until 90 days after filing and service (RCW 26.09.030).
  • Neither spouse can sell community real property without the other's signature (RCW 26.16.030).
  • Transfers between spouses that carry out a divorce settlement are exempt from real estate excise tax (WAC 458-61A-203).
  • Selling to a cash buyer is typically below retail, so it fits best when speed or condition matters more than top price.

How Does Washington Divide a House in a Divorce?

Washington treats most property acquired during a marriage or registered domestic partnership as community property, defined in RCW 26.16.030. Property owned before the marriage, or received by gift or inheritance, is generally separate property. A house can be one, the other, or a mix of both.

Under RCW 26.09.080 the court may divide both community and separate property, "without regard to misconduct." Judges must consider four factors:

  • the nature and extent of the community property;
  • the nature and extent of the separate property;
  • the duration of the marriage or domestic partnership; and
  • each spouse's economic circumstances when the division takes effect, including whether to award the family home, or the right to live there for a reasonable period, to the parent with whom the children mostly live.

In our experience, the longer the marriage and the bigger the gap in earning power, the less likely an even split is. A short marriage where one spouse owned the home beforehand looks very different from a 25-year marriage in a Ballard house bought together.

What If the House Was Mine Before the Marriage?

A home you owned before marriage usually starts as separate property. But it can become partly community if community funds paid down the mortgage, funded a remodel, or the title was changed to both names. Courts look at the facts, and this is the point where couples most often need an attorney to trace the money.

Even a clearly separate house is not off the table, since RCW 26.09.080 lets the court consider separate property when dividing everything. Don't assume that "my name only" settles it.

How Long Does a Divorce Take in Washington?

Under RCW 26.09.030, a court cannot finalize a dissolution until 90 days have passed since the petition was filed and the other spouse was served. That is a minimum. Couples who agree on everything often finish soon after day 90. Contested cases involving a house, a business, or a parenting plan can take a year or more.

The 90 days matters for sellers because it sets the earliest clean date for closing a sale tied to the divorce. If both spouses agree in writing, a listing can often start earlier. Just be sure the signatures and the proceeds split are spelled out before you accept an offer.

Can One Spouse Sell the House Without the Other?

Not if it is community real property. RCW 26.16.030 provides that neither spouse may sell, convey, or encumber community real property unless the other spouse joins in signing the deed, and both must acknowledge it. A buyer's title company will insist on it.

That works both ways: you can't be forced to sign a bad deal, and you can't unilaterally sell, either. If one spouse refuses to cooperate, the divorce court can order the house sold and set how proceeds are divided.

What Taxes Apply When You Divide or Sell a Seattle Home?

Washington real estate excise tax (REET)

Washington charges a graduated state real estate excise tax on sales. The Department of Revenue lists the current state rates as 1.1% on the portion of the price up to $525,000, 1.28% from $525,000 to $1,525,000, 2.75% up to $3,025,000, and 3% above that. The thresholds are scheduled to adjust on January 1, 2027, to $551,000, $1,551,000, and $3,051,000. A local REET set by the city is added on top, so check the Department of Revenue's combined rate table for Seattle before you set a net-proceeds number.

The good news is that a transfer between spouses made to carry out a settlement tied to a divorce decree is exempt under WAC 458-61A-203. You still file the excise tax affidavit with the deed. Transfers between ex-spouses that are not part of the decree's settlement can be taxable, so put the transfer in the decree.

Federal income tax

Under 26 U.S.C. § 1041, a transfer of property between spouses, or between former spouses incident to divorce, is generally not a taxable event. The receiving spouse takes over the original cost basis. When the house later sells, the IRS home-sale exclusion can shield up to $250,000 of gain per person, or $500,000 for a qualifying joint return, if you meet the ownership and use tests. See IRS Publication 523 for the details, including how a spouse who moves out can still count the time the other spouse lives in the home under a divorce agreement.

What About the Mortgage?

Dividing the house does not divide the loan. If both names are on the mortgage and one spouse keeps the house, the lender still holds both spouses responsible unless the loan is refinanced or assumed. A divorce decree binds the two of you, not the bank.

Federal law generally prevents a lender from calling a loan due simply because a home transfers to a spouse as a result of a divorce decree (12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3(d)). That protects the transfer, not your credit. If your ex falls behind, your score takes the hit too. If the spouse keeping the house can't qualify to refinance alone, that alone often pushes a couple toward selling.

Option A: List it with an agent

For a well-kept Seattle home, listing usually produces the highest price. Expect agent commissions, closing costs, state and local excise tax, and a few weeks of showings. If both spouses can cooperate on prep and pricing, this is often the best financial result.

Option B: One spouse buys out the other

This works when one person wants to stay and can refinance in their own name. The buyout is usually the other spouse's share of the equity: market value, minus the mortgage, then divided as agreed or ordered. Get an independent appraisal so neither side argues about the number.

Option C: Keep the house for a set period

Because RCW 26.09.080 allows awarding the right to live in the family home for a reasonable time to the parent with whom the children mostly live, some decrees delay the sale. It gives kids stability, but it keeps both spouses financially linked. Put the end date, who pays what, and what triggers a sale in writing.

Option D: Sell as-is to a cash buyer

A direct cash sale means no repairs, no showings, no appraisal, and a closing date you choose, often within a couple of weeks. The honest trade-off is price: a cash offer is below what a fully renovated, well-marketed home might bring, because the buyer takes on repairs and risk. If the house is in good condition and you have time, listing nets you more. If the house needs work, one spouse has already moved out, or the stress of carrying two households is the bigger cost, certainty can be worth the discount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my spouse stop me from selling our house during a Washington divorce?

Often, yes. If the house is community property, RCW 26.16.030 says neither spouse can sell or encumber it unless the other signs the deed. Once a divorce is filed, the court can also issue orders limiting transfers. In practice, you need your spouse's signature or a court order authorizing the sale.

Is Washington a 50/50 state for dividing the house?

No. Washington is a community property state, but RCW 26.09.080 requires a division that is just and equitable, not automatically equal. Judges weigh the community and separate property, the length of the marriage, and each spouse's economic circumstances. Many couples still land near an even split, but the law does not require it.

Do we owe real estate excise tax if one spouse keeps the house?

Generally not, when the transfer carries out a settlement agreement tied to the divorce decree. Washington Administrative Code 458-61A-203 exempts those transfers between spouses. You still file a real estate excise tax affidavit with the deed. A later sale to a third party is taxable under the normal state and local rates.

How long does a divorce take in Washington if we want to sell the house?

The earliest a divorce can be finalized is 90 days after the petition is filed and served, under RCW 26.09.030. The house itself can often be listed or sold sooner if both spouses agree in writing, though most couples wait for the settlement terms so proceeds are divided cleanly.
